## 1. BASIC PSYCHOLOGY (30 Marks)

### 1.1 Historical Schools & Systems of Psychology
* **Structuralism (Wilhelm Wundt, Edward Titchener):** First formal school (1879, Leipzig). Focused on breaking consciousness down into elementary sensations and feelings using *trained introspection*.
* **Functionalism (William James, John Dewey):** Influenced by Darwinian evolution. Focused on the *function* and adaptive purpose of conscious experience and behavior rather than static structure.
* **Gestalt Psychology (Max Wertheimer, Wolfgang Köhler, Kurt Koffka):** "The whole is greater than the sum of its parts." Focus on perceptual organization, insight learning (Köhler's chimps), and the Phi phenomenon.
* **Psychoanalysis (Sigmund Freud):** Deterministic view centered on unconscious drives, repressed psychosexual conflicts, early childhood experiences, and defense mechanisms.
* **Behaviorism (John B. Watson, B.F. Skinner, Ivan Pavlov):** Rejected consciousness/introspection; psychology as the purely objective, empirical study of observable, measurable stimulus-response ($S-R$) relationships.
* **Humanism (Carl Rogers, Abraham Maslow):** The "Third Force." Emphasizes human agency, free will, self-actualization, inherent goodness, and phenomenological perspective.
* **Cognitive Revolution (Ulric Neisser, George Miller, Noam Chomsky):** Emerged in 1950s–60s; re-introduced internal mental processes (memory, thinking, problem solving) using information-processing metaphors (mind as computer).

> ⚡ **Exam Trap:** Wundt founded the first psychology lab (1879), but Titchener named and formally exported *Structuralism* to the US. William James wrote the seminal *Principles of Psychology* (1890).

### 1.2 Biological Bases of Behavior
* **The Neuron:** Dendrites (receive signals), Soma (cell body), Axon (transmits action potential), Myelin Sheath (Schwann cells/oligodendrocytes; speeds conduction via Nodes of Ranvier / saltatory conduction), Terminal Buttons (release neurotransmitters).
* **Resting & Action Potential:**
  * Resting Membrane Potential: Approx. **$-70\text{ mV}$** ($Na^+$ high outside, $K^+$ high inside, maintained by $Na^+/K^+$ pump: 3 $Na^+$ out for 2 $K^+$ in).
  * Depolarization threshold ($\approx -55\text{ mV}$) triggers **All-or-None** action potential spike ($+30\text{ to }+40\text{ mV}$) via voltage-gated $Na^+$ channels.
* **Neurotransmitters (NTs):**
  * **Acetylcholine (ACh):** Muscle contraction, memory; deficiency linked to Alzheimer's disease.
  * **Dopamine (DA):** Reward, voluntary movement; excess $\rightarrow$ Schizophrenia positive symptoms; deficit $\rightarrow$ Parkinson's disease.
  * **Serotonin (5-HT):** Mood, sleep, hunger; deficit linked to Major Depressive Disorder.
  * **Norepinephrine (NE):** Arousal, fight-or-flight alert; low levels in depression, high in mania.
  * **GABA ($\gamma$-aminobutyric acid):** Primary *inhibitory* NT in CNS; deficits trigger anxiety, seizures, insomnia.
  * **Glutamate:** Primary *excitatory* NT; excess causes excitotoxicity and migraines.
  * **Endorphins:** Endogenous opioids, natural pain relief and euphoria.
* **Brain Anatomy & Hemispheres:**
  * **Hindbrain:** Medulla (autonomic functions: breathing, heart rate), Pons (sleep/arousal bridge), Cerebellum (fine motor coordination, balance, procedural memory).
  * **Midbrain:** Reticular Activating System (RAS) controlling vigilance and sleep-wake cycle; Substantia Nigra (dopamine production).
  * **Forebrain & Limbic System:**
    * *Thalamus:* Sensory relay station for all senses **except olfaction (smell)**.
    * *Hypothalamus:* The 4 Fs (Fighting, Fleeing, Feeding, Mating); homeostasis; controls pituitary gland.
    * *Amygdala:* Emotional processing, specifically fear and aggression.
    * *Hippocampus:* Consolidation of declarative (semantic/episodic) long-term memory.
    * *Basal Ganglia:* Motor control, habit formation.
* **Cerebral Cortex (4 Lobes):**
  * *Frontal:* Executive functioning, motor cortex, Broca’s area (motor speech production, left hemisphere).
  * *Parietal:* Somatosensory cortex (touch, temperature, proprioception), spatial manipulation.
  * *Occipital:* Primary visual cortex ($V1$).
  * *Temporal:* Primary auditory cortex, Wernicke’s area (language comprehension, left hemisphere).

### 1.3 Sensation and Perception
* **Absolute Threshold:** Minimum stimulus intensity detectable 50% of the time.
* **Difference Threshold (Just Noticeable Difference / JND):** Minimum change detectable 50% of the time.
* **Weber's Law:** $\frac{\Delta I}{I} = k$ (JND is a constant proportion of the initial stimulus intensity, not a constant absolute value).
* **Signal Detection Theory (SDT):** Detection depends on sensory sensitivity ($d'$) and decision criteria ($\beta$). Four outcomes: Hit, Miss, False Alarm, Correct Rejection.
* **Sensory Adaptation:** Diminished sensitivity as a consequence of prolonged, unchanging stimulation.
* **Gestalt Laws of Perceptual Grouping:** Proximity, Similarity, Continuity, Closure, Common Fate, Figure-Ground segregation.
* **Depth Cues:**
  * *Monocular:* Linear perspective, relative size, interposition/occlusion, motion parallax, texture gradient, atmospheric perspective.
  * *Binocular:* Retinal disparity (stereopsis), binocular convergence.
* **Theories of Color Vision:**
  * *Trichromatic Theory (Young-Helmholtz):* 3 cone receptors (Red, Green, Blue) at the retinal stage.
  * *Opponent-Process Theory (Hering):* 3 antagonistic color pairs (Red-Green, Blue-Yellow, Black-White) at ganglion/LGN level; explains negative afterimages.

### 1.4 Learning
* **Classical Conditioning (Pavlov):**
  * Unconditioned Stimulus (UCS $\rightarrow$ Food) automatically elicits Unconditioned Response (UCR $\rightarrow$ Salivation).
  * Conditioned Stimulus (CS $\rightarrow$ Bell) paired with UCS.
  * Eventually, CS alone elicits Conditioned Response (CR $\rightarrow$ Salivation).
  * Concepts: Acquisition, Extinction (repeated CS without UCS), Spontaneous Recovery, Stimulus Generalization (Little Albert), Stimulus Discrimination, Higher-Order Conditioning.
* **Operant Conditioning (B.F. Skinner / Thorndike’s Law of Effect):**
  * *Reinforcement (Increases behavior):*
    * Positive Reinforcement: Adding a desirable stimulus.
    * Negative Reinforcement: Removing/avoiding an aversive stimulus (e.g., taking an aspirin, fastening seatbelt).
  * *Punishment (Decreases behavior):*
    * Positive Punishment: Adding an aversive stimulus (e.g., reprimand, electric shock).
    * Negative Punishment (Omission training / Response Cost): Removing a pleasant stimulus (e.g., losing screen privileges).
  * *Schedules of Reinforcement:*
    * Fixed Ratio (FR): Reinforcement after set number of responses (e.g., commission per 5 sales). High response rate with post-reinforcement pause.
    * Variable Ratio (VR): Reinforcement after unpredictable number of responses (e.g., slot machines). **Highest response rate and highest resistance to extinction.**
    * Fixed Interval (FI): Reinforcement for first response after set time (e.g., monthly paycheck, cramming before weekly test). Scalloped response curve.
    * Variable Interval (VI): Reinforcement after unpredictable time intervals (e.g., pop quizzes, checking email). Moderate, steady response rate.
* **Observational Learning (Albert Bandura):**
  * Bobo Doll experiment (1961); showed modeling and vicarious reinforcement.
  * Four essential processes: **Attention $\rightarrow$ Retention $\rightarrow$ Reproduction $\rightarrow$ Motivation**.

### 1.5 Memory and Forgetting
* **Atkinson-Shiffrin Multi-Store Model (Modal Model):**
  * *Sensory Memory:* Huge capacity, brief duration (Iconic $\approx 0.5\text{ s}$; Echoic $\approx 3\text{--}4\text{ s}$).
  * *Short-Term Memory (STM):* Miller's magic number $7 \pm 2$ chunks; updated by Cowan to $4 \pm 1$. Duration $\approx 15\text{--}30\text{ s}$ without rehearsal.
  * *Long-Term Memory (LTM):* Unlimited capacity, theoretically permanent duration.
* **Baddeley & Hitch Working Memory Model:**
  * Replaced passive STM with dynamic working memory.
  * Components: Central Executive, Phonological Loop (acoustic), Visuospatial Sketchpad, Episodic Buffer (integrates modalities + LTM).
* **Types of Long-Term Memory:**
  * *Explicit / Declarative (Conscious):* Semantic (general knowledge/facts) and Episodic (personally experienced events, autobiographical).
  * *Implicit / Non-declarative (Unconscious):* Procedural (motor skills like cycling), Priming, Conditioning.
* **Levels of Processing (Craik & Lockhart):** Depth of processing dictates retention: Structural (shallow) $\rightarrow$ Phonemic (intermediate) $\rightarrow$ Semantic (deep, meaningful elaborative encoding).
* **Theories of Forgetting:**
  * *Decay Theory & Ebbinghaus Curve:* Retention drops steeply in first 24–48 hours, then asymptotes.
  * *Interference Theory:*
    * **Proactive Interference:** Old memories disrupt retrieval of *new* information (e.g., calling your new phone number by your old number).
    * **Retroactive Interference:** New memories disrupt retrieval of *old* information (e.g., learning French makes it hard to recall previously learned Spanish).
  * *Retrieval Failure:* "Tip-of-the-tongue" phenomenon; lack of retrieval cues (Encoding Specificity Principle - Tulving).
  * *Amnesia:* Retrograde (cannot recall past before trauma); Anterograde (cannot form new LTM after trauma; e.g., Patient H.M.).

### 1.6 Cognition, Language & Intelligence
* **Thinking & Problem-Solving:**
  * *Algorithms:* Step-by-step exhaustive procedures that guarantee a solution (slow, resource-intensive).
  * *Heuristics:* Mental shortcuts / rules of thumb (fast, prone to biases).
    * Availability Heuristic: Estimating likelihood based on how readily examples come to mind.
    * Representativeness Heuristic: Judging probability based on similarity to a prototype.
    * Anchoring and Adjustment: Over-relying on the first piece of information encountered.
  * *Obstacles:* Functional Fixedness (inability to see novel uses for familiar objects), Mental Set (persisting in using old strategies that worked previously), Confirmation Bias.
* **Language:**
  * Phonemes (smallest units of sound) $\rightarrow$ Morphemes (smallest units of meaning) $\rightarrow$ Syntax (rules for combining words into sentences) $\rightarrow$ Semantics (meaning).
  * Theories: Skinner (operant reinforcement/imitation) vs. Chomsky (Nativist, Language Acquisition Device / Universal Grammar).
  * Linguistic Relativity (Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis): Language shapes or determines thought.
* **Theories of Intelligence:**
  * *Charles Spearman:* Two-factor theory — general intelligence ($g$) and specific factors ($s$).
  * *Louis Thurstone:* 7 Primary Mental Abilities (verbal comprehension, spatial visualization, etc.).
  * *Raymond Cattell & John Horn:* Fluid Intelligence ($Gf$ - novel problem solving, peaks in young adulthood) vs. Crystallized Intelligence ($Gc$ - accumulated facts/experience, increases across lifespan).
  * *Robert Sternberg (Triarchic Theory):* Analytical, Creative, and Practical intelligence.
  * *Howard Gardner:* Multiple Intelligences (originally 7, now 8–9: linguistic, logical-math, musical, bodily-kinesthetic, spatial, interpersonal, intrapersonal, naturalistic).
  * *Emotional Intelligence (Salovey & Mayer; popularized by Daniel Goleman):* Perceiving, using, understanding, and managing emotions.

### 1.7 Motivation and Emotion
* **Theories of Motivation:**
  * *Instinct Theory (William James, McDougall):* Innate biological instincts drive action.
  * *Drive-Reduction Theory (Clark Hull):* Biological needs create homeostatic imbalance $\rightarrow$ drive state $\rightarrow$ motivated behavior to reduce tension.
  * *Arousal Theory & Yerkes-Dodson Law:* Performance is best at moderate arousal; inverted-U relationship. (Simple tasks require higher arousal; complex tasks require lower arousal).
  * *Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs:* Physiological $\rightarrow$ Safety $\rightarrow$ Love/Belonging $\rightarrow$ Esteem $\rightarrow$ Self-Actualization (Deficiency needs vs. Growth/Being needs).
  * *Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan):* 3 innate psychological needs: Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness.
* **Theories of Emotion:**
  * *James-Lange Theory:* Stimulus $\rightarrow$ Physiological arousal $\rightarrow$ Conscious emotion. ("We feel sad because we cry.")
  * *Cannon-Bard Theory:* Stimulus $\rightarrow$ Simultaneous and independent physiological arousal AND emotional experience (via thalamic routing).
  * *Schachter-Singer Two-Factor Theory:* Stimulus $\rightarrow$ General physiological arousal $\rightarrow$ Cognitive labeling/appraisal based on context $\rightarrow$ Experienced emotion.
  * *Lazarus Cognitive-Mediational Theory:* Cognitive appraisal must occur *before* arousal and emotion.
  * *Facial Feedback Hypothesis:* Facial muscle positioning can influence subjective emotional experience.

### 1.8 Developmental Psychology
* **Jean Piaget’s Stages of Cognitive Development:**
  1. *Sensorimotor (0–2 yrs):* Object permanence, stranger anxiety, circular reactions.
  2. *Preoperational (2–7 yrs):* Symbolic thought, egocentrism, animism, centration, lack of conservation.
  3. *Concrete Operational (7–11 yrs):* Conservation mastered, reversibility, seriation, transitivity, inductive logic.
  4. *Formal Operational (12+ yrs):* Abstract thinking, hypothetical-deductive reasoning.
* **Lev Vygotsky’s Sociocultural Theory:** Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD), Scaffolding (Bruner), private speech as a tool for thought.
* **Erik Erikson’s 8 Psychosocial Stages:**
  1. Trust vs. Mistrust (Infancy - Hope)
  2. Autonomy vs. Shame & Doubt (Toddler - Will)
  3. Initiative vs. Guilt (Preschool - Purpose)
  4. Industry vs. Inferiority (School Age - Competence)
  5. Identity vs. Role Confusion (Adolescence - Fidelity)
  6. Intimacy vs. Isolation (Young Adult - Love)
  7. Generativity vs. Stagnation (Middle Adulthood - Care)
  8. Integrity vs. Despair (Late Adulthood - Wisdom)
* **Lawrence Kohlberg’s Moral Development:**
  * Level 1: Preconventional (Stage 1: Punishment/Obedience; Stage 2: Instrumental/Self-Interest).
  * Level 2: Conventional (Stage 3: Good boy/nice girl conformity; Stage 4: Law and Order).
  * Level 3: Postconventional (Stage 5: Social Contract; Stage 6: Universal Ethical Principles).
  * *Carol Gilligan’s critique:* Kohlberg biased toward masculine justice; women emphasize ethics of care.

### 1.9 Personality
* **Psychodynamic (Freud):**
  * Structure: Id (Pleasure principle, primary process), Ego (Reality principle, secondary process), Superego (Morality principle, ego ideal).
  * Defense Mechanisms: Repression (primary), Denial, Projection, Reaction Formation, Displacement, Sublimation (healthy), Rationalization, Regression, Intellectualization.
* **Neo-Freudians:**
  * *Carl Jung:* Analytical psychology; Personal vs. Collective Unconscious, Archetypes (Shadow, Anima/Animus, Persona, Self).
  * *Alfred Adler:* Individual psychology; Striving for superiority, Inferiority complex, birth order.
  * *Karen Horney:* Basic anxiety, neurotic needs, moving toward / against / away from people; womb envy.
* **Trait Theories:**
  * *Gordon Allport:* Cardinal traits (rare, dominant), Central traits (core characteristics), Secondary traits (situational).
  * *Raymond Cattell:* 16 Personality Factor questionnaire (16PF); Surface traits vs. Source traits; factor analysis.
  * *Hans Eysenck (PEN model):* Psychoticism, Extraversion-Introversion, Neuroticism (biological basis: cortical arousal in ARAS).
  * *The Big Five / Five-Factor Model (Costa & McCrae - OCEAN):*
    * **O**penness to experience (inventive/curious vs. consistent/cautious)
    * **C**onscientiousness (efficient/organized vs. extravagant/careless)
    * **E**xtraversion (outgoing/energetic vs. solitary/reserved)
    * **A**greeableness (friendly/compassionate vs. critical/rational)
    * **N**euroticism (sensitive/nervous vs. resilient/confident)
* **Humanistic Perspective:**
  * *Carl Rogers:* Self-concept, Real self vs. Ideal self (incongruence $\rightarrow$ neurosis), Unconditional Positive Regard (UPR), fully functioning person.

### 1.10 Social Psychology
* **Attributions:**
  * Internal (dispositional) vs. External (situational).
  * *Fundamental Attribution Error (FAE):* Overemphasizing dispositional factors and underestimating situational factors when explaining *others'* behavior.
  * *Actor-Observer Bias:* Attributing own actions to situation, but others' actions to disposition.
  * *Self-Serving Bias:* Attributing our successes to disposition and failures to situation.
* **Social Influence & Conformity:**
  * *Solomon Asch (Line experiment):* Normative social influence (desire for approval) vs. Informational social influence (desire to be accurate).
  * *Stanley Milgram (Obedience experiment):* 65% of participants delivered maximum 450-volt shock under authority pressure.
  * *Philip Zimbardo (Stanford Prison Experiment):* Deindividuation, power of social roles.
* **Group Dynamics:**
  * *Social Facilitation:* Enhanced performance on simple/well-learned tasks in presence of others; *Social Inhibition* on complex/novel tasks (Zajonc's drive theory).
  * *Social Loafing:* Exerting less effort in a group when individual effort is pooled and unidentifiable.
  * *Groupthink (Irving Janis):* Tendency for cohesive groups to prioritize unanimity over realistic appraisal of alternatives.
  * *Group Polarization:* Tendency for group discussions to intensify members' initial inclinations.
  * *Bystander Effect & Diffusion of Responsibility (Darley & Latané):* Murder of Kitty Genovese; presence of others reduces likelihood of individual intervention.
* **Attitudes:** Cognitive Dissonance Theory (Leon Festinger) — psychological discomfort from contradictory beliefs or belief-behavior mismatch, resolved by changing attitude or rationalizing.

### 1.11 Abnormal Psychology (DSM-5 Basics)
* **Anxiety Disorders:** Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD), Panic Disorder (sudden panic attacks), Agoraphobia, Specific Phobias, Social Anxiety Disorder.
* **Obsessive-Compulsive & Related Disorders:** OCD (Obsessions = intrusive distressing thoughts; Compulsions = repetitive ritualistic behaviors to neutralize anxiety).
* **Trauma & Stressor-Related:** PTSD (symptoms $> 1$ month: re-experiencing, hyperarousal, avoidance) vs. Acute Stress Disorder ($< 1$ month).
* **Depressive & Bipolar Disorders:**
  * Major Depressive Disorder (MDD): Depressed mood or anhedonia for $\ge 2$ weeks plus neurovegetative symptoms.
  * Persistent Depressive Disorder (Dysthymia): Chronic depressed mood for $\ge 2$ years.
  * Bipolar I (at least one full manic episode) vs. Bipolar II (hypomanic episode + major depressive episode, no full mania).
  * Cyclothymic Disorder: Fluctuating hypomanic and depressive symptoms for $\ge 2$ years without meeting full criteria.
* **Schizophrenia Spectrum:**
  * Positive Symptoms: Hallucinations (mostly auditory), delusions (persecution, grandeur), disorganized speech/thought.
  * Negative Symptoms: Flat affect, avolition, alogia, anhedonia, asociality.
  * Duration $\ge 6$ months for schizophrenia diagnosis.
* **Personality Disorders (3 Clusters):**
  * *Cluster A (Odd/Eccentric):* Paranoid, Schizoid (detached, indifferent), Schizotypal (magical thinking).
  * *Cluster B (Dramatic/Erratic):* Antisocial (lack of empathy/remorse, deceitful), Borderline (instability in relationships, identity, and affect; fear of abandonment), Histrionic (attention-seeking), Narcissistic (grandiosity, need for admiration).
  * *Cluster C (Anxious/Fearful):* Avoidant, Dependent, Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder (OCPD - perfectionism without distinct OCD obsessions/compulsions).

## 2. RESEARCH APTITUDE (15 Marks)

### 2.1 Scientific Method & Research Designs
* **Types of Research:**
  * *Basic / Fundamental:* Expanding theoretical knowledge without immediate practical application.
  * *Applied:* Solving specific, practical real-world problems.
  * *Action Research:* Cyclical process (Plan $\rightarrow$ Act $\rightarrow$ Observe $\rightarrow$ Reflect) carried out by practitioners to improve local practices.
  * *Exploratory:* Preliminary study when a problem is poorly defined.
  * *Descriptive:* Describing characteristics of a population (surveys, case studies, naturalistic observation; no causation).
* **Experimental Designs:**
  * *True Experiment:* Requires manipulation of IV, control group, and **random assignment** of participants to conditions. Allows causal inference.
  * *Quasi-Experiment:* Manipulates an IV or compares pre-existing groups, but **lacks random assignment** (e.g., comparing two classrooms or gender groups).
  * *Correlational Study:* Measures statistical association between variables (Pearson's $r$: $-1.00$ to $+1.00$). **Correlation does not imply causation!**
  * *Between-Subjects Design:* Different participants assigned to each experimental condition.
  * *Within-Subjects Design (Repeated Measures):* Same participants experience all conditions; vulnerable to *order effects* (controlled via **counterbalancing** / Latin square).

---

### 2.2 Variables and Hypotheses
* **Variables:**
  * *Independent Variable (IV):* The cause / antecedent manipulated by the researcher.
  * *Dependent Variable (DV):* The effect / outcome measured by the researcher.
  * *Extraneous Variable:* Any uncontrolled variable that may influence the DV.
  * *Confounding Variable:* An extraneous variable that covaries systematically with the IV, threatening internal validity.
  * *Mediating Variable (Mediator):* The mechanism/process *through which* the IV affects the DV (answers *why/how*).
  * *Moderating Variable (Moderator):* A variable that influences the *strength or direction* of the IV–DV relationship (answers *for whom / when*).
* **Hypotheses:**
  * *Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):* States there is no relationship or no difference ($\mu_1 = \mu_2$).
  * *Alternative / Research Hypothesis ($H_1$):* Directional (one-tailed) or Non-directional (two-tailed).
* **Statistical Decision Errors:**
  * **Type I Error ($\alpha$):** False Positive. Rejecting $H_0$ when $H_0$ is actually true. (Set by significance level, typically $\alpha = 0.05$).
  * **Type II Error ($\beta$):** False Negative. Failing to reject $H_0$ when $H_0$ is false.
  * **Statistical Power ($1 - \beta$):** The probability of correctly rejecting a false null hypothesis. Increased by larger sample size, larger effect size, and lower measurement error.

---

### 2.3 Sampling Techniques
* **Probability Sampling (Every unit has known, non-zero chance of selection; enables generalizability):**
  * *Simple Random Sampling:* Every member has equal probability (lottery/random number generator).
  * *Systematic Sampling:* Selecting every $k$-th element from a sampling frame ($k = N/n$).
  * *Stratified Random Sampling:* Population divided into homogeneous subgroups (strata based on gender, age, income), then random samples drawn from each stratum (proportionate or disproportionate).
  * *Cluster Sampling:* Population divided into heterogeneous natural clusters (schools, city blocks); entire clusters randomly selected.
  * *Multistage Sampling:* Successive random sampling across levels (e.g., state $\rightarrow$ district $\rightarrow$ school $\rightarrow$ student).
* **Non-Probability Sampling (Units selected based on non-random criteria; prone to selection bias):**
  * *Convenience Sampling:* Readily available participants.
  * *Purposive / Judgmental Sampling:* Participants selected deliberately based on researcher expertise regarding study criteria.
  * *Quota Sampling:* Non-random stratified sampling (filling preset demographic quotas with convenient subjects).
  * *Snowball / Chain-referral Sampling:* Existing participants recruit future subjects; ideal for hidden or hard-to-reach populations (e.g., substance users, rare disease patients).

---

### 2.4 Measurement, Reliability & Validity
* **Scales of Measurement (NOIR - Stevens, 1946):**
  * *Nominal:* Categorical labels with no numerical order (e.g., gender, blood type, jersey numbers). Mode is only valid central tendency.
  * *Ordinal:* Ranked order, but unequal intervals (e.g., Likert scales, race finish positions 1st/2nd/3rd). Median is appropriate.
  * *Interval:* Ordered, equal intervals, but **no true zero point** (arbitrary zero; e.g., Celsius/Fahrenheit temperature, standard IQ scores). Mean and SD are valid; cannot calculate ratios.
  * *Ratio:* Ordered, equal intervals, **with a true absolute zero point** (e.g., reaction time in ms, weight, salary, number of errors). All math operations valid.
* **Reliability (Consistency & Stability of Measurement):**
  * *Test-Retest:* Temporal stability across two time points (Pearson's $r$).
  * *Parallel/Alternate Forms:* Consistency across two equivalent versions of a test.
  * *Split-Half:* Internal consistency splitting test into odd/even items; corrected using **Spearman-Brown prophecy formula**.
  * *Cronbach’s Alpha ($\alpha$):* Mean of all possible split-half correlations; values $\ge 0.70$ acceptable, $\ge 0.80$ good.
  * *Inter-Rater Reliability:* Agreement between multiple scorers (Cohen's Kappa $\kappa$).
* **Validity (Accuracy of Measurement — does it measure what it purports to measure?):**
  * *Content Validity:* Degree to which test items comprehensively cover the intended domain (established via expert panel / CVI).
  * *Criterion-Related Validity:*
    * *Concurrent:* Test correlates with an established criterion measured simultaneously.
    * *Predictive:* Test score predicts future performance on a criterion (e.g., entrance score predicting college GPA).
  * *Construct Validity:* Most fundamental validity; degree to which a test measures a theoretical construct:
    * *Convergent Validity:* High correlation with other tests measuring the same/similar construct.
    * *Discriminant / Divergent Validity:* Low correlation with tests measuring unrelated constructs.
  * *Internal Validity:* Certainty that IV caused the change in DV (threatened by history, maturation, testing, regression to the mean, attrition).
  * *External Validity:* Generalizability of findings across populations, settings, and times.

---

### 2.5 Descriptive and Inferential Statistics
* **Measures of Central Tendency:** Mean (arithmetic average, sensitive to extreme outliers), Median (middle value, best for skewed distributions), Mode (most frequent score).
  * *Positive (Right) Skew:* Tail to the right; $\text{Mode} < \text{Median} < \text{Mean}$.
  * *Negative (Left) Skew:* Tail to the left; $\text{Mean} < \text{Median} < \text{Mode}$.
  * *Normal Curve:* Symmetrical, bell-shaped; $\text{Mean} = \text{Median} = \text{Mode}$.
    * Empirical Rule: $68.26\%$ within $\pm 1\sigma$; $95.44\%$ within $\pm 2\sigma$; $99.74\%$ within $\pm 3\sigma$.
* **Measures of Dispersion:** Range, Variance ($\sigma^2$ or $s^2$), Standard Deviation ($\sigma$ or $s$), Interquartile Range (IQR = $Q_3 - Q_1$).
* **Standard Scores:** $Z$-score = $\frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}$ (mean = 0, SD = 1); $T$-score = $10Z + 50$ (mean = 50, SD = 10).
* **Parametric vs. Non-Parametric Tests:**
  * *Parametric Assumptions:* Normal distribution, interval/ratio data, homogeneity of variance.
    * Independent Samples $t$-test: Compares means of 2 unrelated groups.
    * Paired/Dependent $t$-test: Compares means of 2 related/matched groups or pre-post test.
    * One-Way ANOVA ($F$-test): Compares means across $\ge 3$ groups (avoids compounding Type I error rate of multiple $t$-tests).
    * Factorial ANOVA: $\ge 2$ independent variables; assesses main effects and interaction effects.
    * Pearson’s $r$: Linear correlation between two continuous variables.
  * *Non-Parametric Equivalents (for ordinal/nominal data or violated normality):*
    * Mann-Whitney $U$ test $\leftrightarrow$ Independent $t$-test.
    * Wilcoxon Signed-Rank test $\leftrightarrow$ Paired $t$-test.
    * Kruskal-Wallis test $\leftrightarrow$ One-Way ANOVA.
    * Spearman’s Rank Correlation ($\rho$) $\leftrightarrow$ Pearson’s $r$.
    * Chi-Square ($\chi^2$) Goodness-of-Fit / Independence: Categorical data frequencies.

## 3. ETHICAL CONCERNS (15 Marks)

### 3.1 APA Ethics Code: 5 General Principles (Aspirational)
1. **Principle A: Beneficence and Nonmaleficence:** Strive to benefit those with whom they work and take care to do no harm. Safeguard welfare and rights.
2. **Principle B: Fidelity and Responsibility:** Establish relationships of trust; clarify roles and responsibilities; manage conflicts of interest; uphold professional standards.
3. **Principle C: Integrity:** Promote accuracy, honesty, and truthfulness in the science, teaching, and practice of psychology. Avoid fraud, subterfuge, or misrepresentation.
4. **Principle D: Justice:** Ensure all persons have equal access to and benefit from the contributions of psychology; avoid unfair practices or bias.
5. **Principle E: Respect for People's Rights and Dignity:** Respect dignity, worth, privacy, confidentiality, self-determination, and cultural/individual diversity.

### 3.2 Key Ethical Standards (Enforceable Rules)
* **Informed Consent (Standard 8.02):**
  * Must inform participants of: Purpose, expected duration, and procedures; Right to decline and withdraw without penalty; Potential risks, discomforts, or adverse effects; Prospective research benefits; Limits of confidentiality; Incentives for participation; Contact details for inquiries.
  * For minors or legally incapacitated individuals: Obtain legal guardian's informed consent AND child's affirmative **assent**.
* **Deception in Research (Standard 8.07):**
  * Prohibited unless: (1) Justified by significant prospective scientific/educational value, AND (2) Effective nondeceptive alternatives are not feasible.
  * **Strict absolute rule:** Psychologists **NEVER** deceive prospective participants about research that is reasonably expected to cause physical pain or severe emotional distress.
* **Debriefing (Standard 8.08):**
  * Obligation to provide a prompt opportunity for participants to obtain information about the nature, results, and conclusions of the research.
  * If deception was used, the true purpose must be explained as early as feasible (usually immediately after participation).
* **Confidentiality & Privileged Communication (Standard 4):**
  * Duty to protect participant and client data.
  * *Exceptions to Confidentiality:*
    * Imminent risk of suicide or homicide (Duty to Warn / Duty to Protect: *Tarasoff v. Regents of the University of California*, 1976).
    * Suspected child abuse, elder abuse, or abuse of dependent adults (mandatory reporting laws).
    * Valid court subpoena from a judge.
* **Multiple / Dual Relationships (Standard 3.05):**
  * Psychologists refrain from entering into a multiple relationship if it could reasonably be expected to impair objectivity, competence, or effectiveness, or risk exploitation or harm.
  * Sexual intimate relationships with current therapy clients are strictly prohibited (and for at least 2 years post-termination under stringent conditions).
* **Scientific Integrity & Publication Ethics:**
  * Fabrication (inventing data) and Falsification (manipulating research materials or omitting data).
  * Plagiarism: Presenting portions of another's work or data as one's own without attribution.
  * Duplicate publication of data without acknowledgment.
  * Authorship: Accurately reflects substantial scientific contributions (not merely administrative or funding support).

## 4. GENERAL APTITUDE (15 Marks)

### 4.1 Quantitative Formulas & Concepts
* **Percentages:** $\%\text{ change} = \frac{\text{New} - \text{Old}}{\text{Old}} \times 100$. Successive percentage changes $a\%$ and $b\%$: Net change $= a + b + \frac{ab}{100}\%$.
* **Ratios & Proportions:** If $A:B = 3:4$ and $B:C = 5:6$, then $A:B:C = (3 \times 5):(4 \times 5):(4 \times 6) = 15:20:24$.
* **Averages:** $\text{Average} = \frac{\text{Sum of terms}}{\text{Number of terms}}$. If the average of $n$ quantities increases by $x$ when a new item replaces an old one: $\text{New} = \text{Old} + n \times x$.
* **Profit, Loss & Discount:** $\text{Profit} = SP - CP$; $\text{Profit } \% = \frac{\text{Profit}}{CP} \times 100$. Discount is always calculated on Marked Price ($MP$): $SP = MP \times (1 - d\%)$.
* **Time, Speed & Distance:** $\text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time}$. Convert $\text{km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$ by multiplying by $\frac{5}{18}$. Average speed for same distance at speeds $x$ and $y = \frac{2xy}{x+y}$.
* **Time & Work:** If A does work in $x$ days and B in $y$ days, together they take $\frac{xy}{x+y}$ days.

### 4.2 Logical Reasoning
* **Syllogisms:** Deductive rules using Venn diagrams (All A are B; Some B are C; No C is D). Beware the fallacy of undistributed middle.
* **Series Completion:** Number sequences (differences of squares, Fibonacci, prime numbers, alternating geometric progressions) and alphabetical letter positional values ($A=1, Z=26$; $EJOTY = 5, 10, 15, 20, 25$).
* **Coding-Decoding:** Letter shifts ($+1, -2$), reverse alphabetical pairing ($A \leftrightarrow Z, B \leftrightarrow Y, C \leftrightarrow X$).
* **Blood Relations:** Tree diagrams; maternal vs. paternal uncles/aunts; generational tiers.
* **Direction Sense:** Compass coordinates (North, South, East, West); right/left turns; Pythagoras theorem ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$) for shortest displacement.

### 4.3 Verbal Ability
* **Sentence Correction & Grammar:** Subject-verb agreement, dangling modifiers, parallelism, conditional tenses, correct preposition usage.
* **Reading Comprehension:** Main idea extraction, tone identification (objective, critical, laudatory, pessimistic), direct inferences vs. explicit claims.
* **Contextual Vocabulary:** Synonyms, antonyms, idioms, analogies.

## 6. HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT (15 Marks)

### 6.1 HR Planning & Job Analysis
* **Strategic Human Resource Management (SHRM):** Aligning human capital strategies with overarching corporate business strategy.
* **Human Resource Planning (HRP):** Forecasting demand (trend analysis, Delphi technique) and supply (succession planning, Markov analysis, skills inventory).
* **Job Analysis:** The foundational procedure for collecting information about job requirements.
  * **Job Description (JD):** Statement of *tasks, duties, responsibilities, working conditions, and reporting relationships* (the job itself).
  * **Job Specification (JS):** Statement of *qualifications, skills, knowledge, abilities, and personal traits (KSAOs)* required in the person executing the job.
* **Job Design Approaches:** Job Simplification, Job Rotation, Job Enlargement (horizontal loading), Job Enrichment (vertical loading / adding autonomy and responsibility - Herzberg).

---

### 6.2 Talent Acquisition & Selection
* **Recruitment Funnel:** Sourcing $\rightarrow$ Screening $\rightarrow$ Selection $\rightarrow$ Offer $\rightarrow$ Onboarding.
  * Internal sources (promotions, transfers) vs. External sources (campus placements, headhunters, job boards). Yield ratio calculation.
* **Selection Process & Testing:**
  * Cognitive Ability Tests (Wonderlic, Raven's matrices), Personality Tests (Big Five, MBTI), Work Sample Tests, Assessment Centres.
* **Interview Techniques:**
  * *Unstructured:* Casual, low reliability and predictive validity.
  * *Structured:* Standardized questions, predefined rating scales (high validity).
  * *Behavioral Description Interview (BDI):* "Tell me about a time when you..." (Past behavior predicts future behavior; **STAR technique: Situation, Task, Action, Result**).
  * *Situational Interview:* "What would you do if..." (Future hypothetical scenario).
* **Interviewer Biases:**
  * *Halo Effect:* One positive trait influences overall positive impression.
  * *Horns Effect:* One negative trait colors entire evaluation.
  * *Contrast Effect:* Evaluation influenced by preceding candidate.
  * *First-Impression Error (Primacy Effect):* Forming judgment within first 2–3 minutes.
  * *Similar-to-Me Effect:* Favoring candidates sharing demographic or personal traits.

---

### 6.3 Training & Development
* **Training Needs Analysis (TNA):** 3-level diagnosis (Organizational analysis $\rightarrow$ Task/Operational analysis $\rightarrow$ Person analysis).
* **ADDIE Instructional Design Model:**
  * **A**nalysis $\rightarrow$ **D**esign $\rightarrow$ **D**evelopment $\rightarrow$ **I**mplementation $\rightarrow$ **E**valuation.
* **Kirkpatrick’s 4-Level Training Evaluation Model:**
  1. *Level 1: Reaction:* How did learners react? (Post-training satisfaction surveys / "smile sheets").
  2. *Level 2: Learning:* Did knowledge, skills, or attitudes increase? (Pre- and post-tests).
  3. *Level 3: Behavior:* Did behavior change on the job? (Observed transfer of learning over 3–6 months).
  4. *Level 4: Results:* Did the training impact business metrics? (Sales increase, reduced defect rate, cost savings, ROI).
* **Training Methods:** On-the-job (apprenticeship, coaching, job rotation) vs. Off-the-job (simulations, vestibule training, role-playing, e-learning).

---

### 6.4 Performance Management Systems (PMS)
* **Performance Appraisal Methods:**
  * *Management by Objectives (MBO - Peter Drucker):* Collaborative goal-setting with SMART objectives (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound); evaluation against results.
  * *360-Degree Feedback:* Multi-rater feedback gathered from supervisors, peers, subordinates, and self (and sometimes customers).
  * *Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales (BARS):* Combines rating scales with critical incident behavioral anchors, reducing rating subjectivity.
  * *Forced Distribution Method (Bell Curve):* Raters must distribute employees across preset performance categories (e.g., top 10%, middle 70%, bottom 10%; popularized by Jack Welch at GE).
  * *Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton):* Evaluates performance across 4 perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Business Processes, and Learning & Growth.
* **Appraisal Errors:** Central Tendency (rating everyone as average), Leniency/Strictness, Recency Effect (weighing only recent months' performance).

### 6.5 Compensation, Benefits & Employee Relations
* **Job Evaluation:** Systematically determining the relative worth of jobs within an organization (Internal Equity):
  * Ranking Method, Classification/Grading Method, Factor Comparison, Point-Factor Method (most widely used).
* **Compensation Components:** Base pay, incentives/bonuses, allowances, stock options (ESOPs), statutory benefits (Provident Fund, Gratuity, ESI).
* **Employee Relations & Industrial Disputes:** Grievance redressal procedures, discipline management, collective bargaining stages, works committees.

## 7. INDUSTRIAL & ORGANISATIONAL (I/O) PSYCHOLOGY (15 Marks)

### 7.1 Foundations & History of I/O Psychology
* **Scientific Management (Frederick Winslow Taylor):** "One best way" to perform work; time-and-motion studies, financial incentives as sole motivator.
* **Hugo Münsterberg:** Father of industrial psychology; applied experimental psychology to vocational selection and advertising (*Psychology and Industrial Efficiency*, 1913).
* **Hawthorne Studies (Elton Mayo & Fritz Roethlisberger, 1924–1932):**
  * Investigated lighting, rest breaks, and incentives at Western Electric’s Hawthorne Works.
  * **The Hawthorne Effect:** Workers' productivity increased not because of physical environmental changes, but because of **the novel attention paid to them by researchers and management**.
  * Birthed the **Human Relations Movement** in management.

---

### 7.2 Work Motivation Theories
* **Herzberg’s Two-Factor (Motivator-Hygiene) Theory:**
  * *Hygiene Factors (Extrinsic):* Working conditions, salary, company policies, supervision, interpersonal relations. Presence prevents dissatisfaction, but *cannot create satisfaction or motivation*.
  * *Motivator Factors (Intrinsic):* Achievement, recognition, challenging work, responsibility, advancement. Presence creates true satisfaction and high motivation.
* **Vroom’s Expectancy Theory ($VIE$ Formula):**
  $$\text{Motivation} = \text{Expectancy} \times \text{Instrumentality} \times \text{Valence}$$
  * *Expectancy ($E \rightarrow P$):* Belief that effort will lead to successful performance ($0 \text{ to } 1$).
  * *Instrumentality ($P \rightarrow O$):* Belief that performance will lead to specific outcomes/rewards ($0 \text{ to } 1$).
  * *Valence ($V$):* Value the individual places on that outcome (positive, zero, or negative).
  * **Crucial rule:** If any one component is zero, Motivation is ZERO!
* **Adams’ Equity Theory:**
  * Employees compare their ratio of inputs (effort, education, time) to outcomes (pay, praise) with the input/outcome ratio of a **referent other**:
    $$\frac{\text{Outcomes}_A}{\text{Inputs}_A} \quad \text{vs.} \quad \frac{\text{Outcomes}_B}{\text{Inputs}_B}$$
  * Under-reward inequity triggers anger; over-reward inequity triggers guilt; employees restore equity by altering inputs, changing referent, distorting perceptions, or quitting.
* **Locke & Latham’s Goal-Setting Theory:**
  * Specific, difficult (challenging yet achievable) goals lead to higher performance than easy goals or vague "do your best" instructions.
  * Requires goal commitment, task feedback, and adequate self-efficacy.
* **Hackman & Oldham’s Job Characteristics Model (JCM):**
  * 5 Core Job Dimensions: Skill Variety, Task Identity, Task Significance, Autonomy, and Feedback.
  * Lead to Critical Psychological States (Experienced Meaningfulness, Experienced Responsibility, Knowledge of Results) $\rightarrow$ High Internal Work Motivation and Job Performance.

---

### 7.3 Job Attitudes & Workplace Behaviors
* **Job Satisfaction:** An evaluative state representing feelings about one's job. Measured via Job Descriptive Index (JDI) or Minnesota Satisfaction Questionnaire (MSQ).
* **Organizational Commitment (Meyer & Allen’s Three-Component Model):**
  1. *Affective Commitment:* Emotional attachment to and identification with the organization ("I stay because I **want** to").
  2. *Continuance Commitment:* Awareness of the costs associated with leaving ("I stay because I **need** to").
  3. *Normative Commitment:* Feeling of moral obligation to remain ("I stay because I **ought** to").
* **Positive vs. Deviant Workplace Behaviors:**
  * **Organizational Citizenship Behavior (OCB):** Discretionary, extra-role behaviors not part of formal job duties that promote organizational functioning (altruism, conscientiousness, courtesy, sportsmanship, civic virtue).
  * **Counterproductive Work Behavior (CWB):** Intentional behavior that harms or intends to harm the organization or its members (theft, sabotage, absenteeism, workplace bullying/incivility).

---

### 7.4 Leadership Theories
* **Trait Theories ("Great Man"):** Focus on personal qualities (intelligence, extraversion, emotional intelligence, integrity).
* **Behavioral Theories:**
  * *Ohio State Studies:* Initiating Structure (task-oriented) vs. Consideration (people-oriented).
  * *University of Michigan Studies:* Production-oriented vs. Employee-oriented.
  * *Blake & Mouton’s Managerial Grid:* Concern for Production (1 to 9) vs. Concern for People (1 to 9). Team Management (9,9) considered optimal.
* **Contingency / Situational Theories:**
  * *Fiedler’s Contingency Model:* Leadership style is fixed; measured by **Least Preferred Coworker (LPC)** scale. High LPC = relationship-oriented; Low LPC = task-oriented. Task-oriented leaders perform best in very favorable or very unfavorable situational control; Relationship-oriented leaders perform best in moderate control.
  * *Hersey & Blanchard’s Situational Leadership Theory (SLT):* Matches leadership style (Directing/Telling, Coaching/Selling, Supporting/Participating, Delegating) to the **follower readiness / maturity** level ($R1$ to $R4$).
  * *Path-Goal Theory (Robert House):* Leader's job is to assist followers in attaining goals and clarify the path. Styles: Directive, Supportive, Participative, Achievement-Oriented.
  * *Leader-Member Exchange (LMX) Theory:* Leaders form unique dyadic relationships with subordinates, creating an **In-Group** (high trust, autonomy) and an **Out-Group** (formal, transactional).
* **Transformational vs. Transactional Leadership:**
  * *Transactional:* Contingent reward, Management by Exception (active/passive).
  * *Transformational (The 4 Is - Bass):*
    1. Idealized Influence (Charisma / Role model)
    2. Inspirational Motivation (Articulating compelling vision)
    3. Intellectual Stimulation (Challenging assumptions, encouraging innovation)
    4. Individualized Consideration (Mentoring, personal coaching)

---

### 7.5 Organizational Culture, Change & Workplace Well-Being
* **Edgar Schein’s Three Levels of Organizational Culture:**
  1. *Artifacts:* Visible organizational structures, logos, dress codes, rituals (surface level, easy to observe, hard to decipher).
  2. *Espoused Values:* Stated strategies, goals, philosophies, credos.
  3. *Basic Underlying Assumptions:* Unconscious, taken-for-granted beliefs, habits of perception and thoughts (deepest core level, ultimate source of values and actions).
* **Kurt Lewin’s 3-Step Change Model:**
  * **Unfreezing:** Breaking down existing status quo; creating psychological safety and urgency.
  * **Changing / Moving:** Implementing the new behaviors, processes, and structures.
  * **Refreezing:** Solidifying the change into company culture, norms, and reward systems.
* **Workplace Stress & Occupational Health:**
  * *Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) Model:* Strain occurs when job demands exceed available job resources.
  * *Burnout (Christina Maslach - MBI):* 3 core dimensions:
    1. Emotional Exhaustion (depletion of emotional energy)
    2. Depersonalization / Cynicism (cynical, detached attitude toward clients/work)
    3. Reduced Personal Accomplishment (feelings of incompetence and lack of achievement).
  * *Ergonomics / Human Factors:* Designing equipment, workstations, and interfaces to fit the human body and cognitive processing limits.
